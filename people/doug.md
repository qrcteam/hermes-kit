## The one job

> *"I want to know what I was working on."*

Oz's dad. Builds web projects, self-manages, works in bursts with gaps between them. The gap is
the problem: he comes back to something after a week or three and has to reconstruct where he
left off from the files themselves.

**This is the cleanest use case of the three** — a project journal that answers back. It's also
the easiest to demo, which makes it the best second install.

## Notes for this install

- **The digest is the product for Doug.** Not a task list — a "where you left off" recap:

  > *Read `/vault/wiki/topics/projects/` and any notes from the last 7 days. Remind me what I
  > was working on and where I left off. Lead with anything I said I'd do and haven't. Two or
  > three things, plain sentences. Nothing outstanding? Say so in one line.*

- **Seed it heavily on day one.** He has live projects right now. Spend twenty minutes getting
  the current state of each one in. An empty vault gives him nothing on day two, and day two is
  when he decides whether this is real.
- **Teach the end-of-session habit.** *"Tell it what you just did before you close the laptop."*
  One line, and it's what makes the next morning's digest worth reading.
- **If he's on Windows**, this run is the proof of [`03-runbook-windows.md`](../docs/03-runbook-windows.md).
  Watch for the two-`.hermes` trap especially — run the `PROOF` check, don't assume.
- Keep the whole thing low-ceremony. He's not looking for a system, he's looking for an answer
  to one question.
