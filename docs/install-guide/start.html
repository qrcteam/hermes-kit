Before we build your assistant

Six accounts, twenty minutes

This is everything to set up before our call. Sign up, and stop there — don't create keys, projects or anything clever inside them. We do that part together, on your screen, so nothing lands in the wrong place.

Start here

Use one email address for all six — whichever you think of as your main business email — and keep the passwords wherever you normally keep passwords.

Four of these are free. One costs about $20 a month, and it's the only ongoing cost in the whole setup. One takes a $10 top-up that lasts a very long time.

Every account is in your name, on your card. That's deliberate: if you and I stopped working together tomorrow, your assistant would keep running and you'd keep everything.

The six

Tick them off as you go — this page remembers where you got to.

0 of 6 doneabout 20 minutes, all in

  1. Telegram

    Free

    The doorway — the app you'll actually text your assistant in, the same way you text anyone else. Install it on your phone and make an account. Don't set up the assistant itself yet; that's a two-minute job we do together on the call, on your phone.

    Without it, there's no way to reach it. telegram.org ↗

  2. ChatGPT Plus or Claude Pro

    $20 / month

    The thinking part. This one has to be in your name on your card — it's your account and your own relationship with the AI, not mine. If you already pay for one of them, that's the one we'll use. If you have neither and don't mind which, take ChatGPT Plus; it's the smoother path on the day.

    Without it, nothing thinks. chatgpt.com ↗ claude.ai ↗

  3. GitHub

    Free

    The backup. Every time your assistant learns something, a private copy goes here automatically. It's what makes a dead laptop cost you a laptop instead of everything you've taught it. Sign up, confirm the email, stop there.

    Without it, your memory lives on one disk. github.com/signup ↗

  4. Pinecone

    Free

    This is what lets you ask a question in your own words. Search for “who did I hire for the bathroom?” and it finds the note that says “went with Ruiz” — no matching words at all. The free tier is genuinely enough for one person.

    Without it, you can only search for exact words. pinecone.io ↗

  5. OpenRouter

    ~$10 once

    The understudy. It runs small background jobs and steps in on the days your main AI has an outage. Sign up and put $10 of credit on it — in practice that lasts months, because it's only doing the quiet work.

    Without it, an outage means a silent assistant. openrouter.ai ↗

  6. Obsidian

    Free — no account

    Not an account at all, just an app to download. It's the window you'll read your assistant's memory through — everything it knows about you, as ordinary pages you can open, correct, or delete.

    Without it, the memory works but stays invisible. obsidian.md ↗

Bring your GoHighLevel login. We'll connect it so your assistant can read what's in there and answer questions about it.

Worth checking today

You'll need to reach the settings area where API keys live. If someone else originally set up your account, make sure you can get in there yourself — that's much easier to sort out today than in the middle of our call.

On your Mac

Two things about the machine itself. Neither needs doing before the call, but both are worth knowing now.

It has to stay awake. Your assistant can only answer while your Mac is on and awake, so we'll set it to never sleep while it's plugged in. The screen going dark is fine — the machine going to sleep is not.

There's one piece of software we install together. It takes about five minutes and there's nothing for you to do beforehand. Just have your Mac password to hand, and be plugged in.

What to bring

  • Your phone, with Telegram installed and you already logged in
  • Your Mac, plugged in, with your Mac password to hand
  • The six logins above, plus your GoHighLevel login
  • About ninety minutes — most of which is me asking you questions, not typing

What I'm not asking you for: no keys, no passwords, nothing emailed to me. Every key gets created by you, on your own screen, while we're on the call. You own all six accounts, and you can revoke my access to any of it in one click.
