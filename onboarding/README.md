# Onboarding docs

One per person, adapted from [`onboarding-template.md`](onboarding-template.md).

Copy it to `<name>.md`, fill in every `<bracket>`, cut what doesn't apply, and hand it over
**with nothing else**. If they come back with a question the doc should have answered, that's
an edit to make — not just an answer to give.

## Rules

- **Two pages maximum.** A shorter one they read beats a complete one they don't.
- **No jargon at all.** The words *Docker*, *container*, *Pinecone*, *git*, *repo*, *index* and
  *sync* must not appear. They don't need them and they'd only worry.
- **Write the "what it's for you" line last**, once you've done their SOUL interview and you
  know what they actually want out of it. It's the line that decides whether they try it.
- **Say who to text when it breaks.** Every time.

## Tone, per person

| | Pitch it as |
|---|---|
| **Mazíx** | A place for ideas to land so they don't evaporate. Not a system, not admin. Lead with capture; the daily note is optional |
| **Doug** | *"What was I working on?"* — a project journal that answers back. Lead with the morning note and asking it questions |
| **Diane** | A business memory. She already runs her own AI sessions, so she'll want to know where the boundary is: her machine, her notes, her accounts |

## After handover

Check in at day five with one question: **"has it remembered anything useful yet?"**

If the answer is no, it's almost always a thin `SOUL.md` or buckets that don't match how they
think. Both take ten minutes to fix in week one, and become permanent if you leave them.
