## What it is

It's an assistant that remembers things.

Unlike ChatGPT, it doesn't start over every time you talk to it. Tell it something today and
it'll still know next month. It keeps everything in a folder of notes on your own computer —
notes you can open and read yourself.

<One sentence on what it's for *them*. This is the important line — write it in their language:
"It's for keeping track of what you're working on, so you don't lose the thread when you come
back to something after a week.">

---

## Three things to do this week

**1. Tell it things.** Just text it like you'd text a person.

> *remember the Henderson deposit is due on the 20th*
> *remember Sarah at Wexford prefers email, don't call her*
> *I decided against the Squarespace move — the booking plugin doesn't work*

You don't have to say "remember" — it usually works it out — but saying it makes sure.

**2. Ask it things.** This is the half people forget.

> *when's the Henderson deposit due?*
> *what did I decide about Squarespace?*
> *what was I working on last week?*

**3. Correct it when it's wrong.**

> *no, the deposit's the 20th, not the 12th*

It'll fix its notes. **Please actually do this** — most people just sigh and move on, and then
the wrong thing stays wrong forever.

- **It'll say "I don't know."** That's deliberate too. Better than a confident guess.

## What it can't do

Worth knowing up front so nothing's a surprise:

- **It can only reply when your computer is on and awake.** If your laptop's shut, it'll get
  your message when the machine wakes up.
- **It's not connected to your email or calendar** <*(adjust if it is)*>. It only knows what
  you tell it and what's in your notes.
- **It can be wrong.** It's a very good assistant, not an oracle. If something matters, check
  it. It's honest about not knowing, but it can still get things confidently wrong — same as
  any AI.
- **It can't do things for you** — no sending emails, no booking things. It remembers, thinks
  and answers.

## If something's wrong

**It's not replying** → check your computer is on and awake. That's it, nine times out of ten.
Still nothing after a few minutes, text <OPERATOR>.

**It's forgotten something you told it** → tell <OPERATOR>. Don't just work around it — that
means something's broken and it's fixable.

**It's being annoying** → tell <OPERATOR> exactly what it did. Almost everything about how it
behaves can be changed: how long its answers are, when it messages you, whether it argues with
you. It's meant to fit you, not the other way round.

Give it two weeks.

The first few days it'll feel like texting a stranger, because it is one. It gets noticeably
better once it's got a bit of your life written down — and it only gets that by you telling it
things.

The people who get the most out of this are the ones who tell it small stuff constantly, not
the ones who wait for something important enough to be worth saving.
